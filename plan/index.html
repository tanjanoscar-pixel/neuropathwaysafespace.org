The Plan for Change

What we're building, because of what we lived through

Our journey showed us exactly where the system breaks down for families. This is the plan that came out of it — NeuroPathway, and the changes behind it.

The problem, plainly

Across the UK, families like ours face delayed identification, fragmented communication between services, long EHCP evidence timelines, emotional burnout, and rising exclusions. None of that is because any one professional doesn't care. It's because there's no shared, structured way for a parent's daily observations to become evidence a school, a GP, and a local authority can all act on together.

NeuroPathway is our answer: a needs-first, not labels-first, digital layer that strengthens the services already in place — it doesn't replace them.

The plan, step by step

1

Catch it earlier

Turn everyday behaviours and wellbeing patterns — the things parents already notice first — into structured, dated observations, instead of relying on memory in a five-minute appointment.

2

Cut the evidence-gathering time

Generate clear, EHCP-ready summaries and printable reports directly from tracked observations, so families aren't rebuilding the same case from scratch for every service.

3

Join up the services around the child

Give teachers, parents, and professionals role-based access to the same picture, with risk colour-coding, so no one is acting on partial information.

4

Give children a voice too

"Your Safe Space" — a companion app for children and young people aged 8–17 — for emotional check-ins, safe journaling, and wellbeing tracking, so the record includes how she experiences things, not just how adults observe her.

5

Reduce crisis and exclusion

Surface patterns early enough that support can happen before a crisis point, instead of after an exclusion or a safeguarding incident forces the system to react.

6

Make it last a lifetime

Support the same person's record from childhood through adulthood, so nobody has to start their story over from zero at every transition — school to college, child to adult services.

Where the plan is headed

  1. Phase 1

    School pilot

    Testing the tool with real teachers, parents, and children in a small number of schools.

  2. Phase 2

    Local authority & NHS alignment

    Working with local authorities and NHS teams so evidence generated by families is trusted and usable in official processes.

  3. Phase 3

    Regional scaling

    Expanding beyond the pilot to more schools and services across a region.

  4. Phase 4

    National rollout

    Making the approach available to families and services nationally.

  5. Phase 5

    Cross-sector integration

    Extending the same approach into social care, justice, and housing — everywhere a child's story currently gets told from scratch, again and again.

This plan needs more than one family behind it

We're looking to work with schools, SEND teams, NHS trusts, ICBs, researchers, and other parents who've lived a version of this story. If any of that is you, we want to hear from you.
